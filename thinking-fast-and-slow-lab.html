Daniel Kahneman · Thinking, Fast and Slow · fourteen small experiments

Your head has two ways of thinking. Catch each one at work.

Kahneman calls them System 1 and System 2. System 1 is fast and automatic. It reads this sentence, sees a face, and hands you 2 + 2 before you ask. System 2 is slow and careful. It multiplies 17 by 24, checks an argument, and would rather not. Every station below is one of the small experiments from the book, done on your own head. Before you start, write down a guess: at how many of the fourteen stations will System 1 get its way?

System 1 · fast

Answers before you ask

  • Automatic. No effort, no sense of control.
  • Always on. You cannot switch it off.
  • Works by resemblance, habit and feel.
  • Jumps to a conclusion and hides the doubt.
  • Right most of the time. Wrong in set ways.

Reads a word. Sees anger in a face. Drives on an empty road. 2 + 2.

System 2 · slow

Works when it is called

  • Effortful. You feel yourself doing it.
  • Lazy. Accepts System 1 unless something looks wrong.
  • Works by rules, steps and checking.
  • Can hold a rule: check the arithmetic, find the base rate.
  • The only one of the two that can doubt.

17 × 24. Parks in a tight space. Fills a form. Checks whether an argument follows.

2 + 2 = ? The answer was there before you finished reading. System 1
Neither one is the villain. System 1 keeps you alive. System 2 is the only one that can check its work.

Fourteen stations, in the order of the book

The stations · the whole tour takes about thirty minutes

Fourteen small experiments from the book.

Each station is one demonstration. A card tells you what to do, you do it, and the page shows you what happened and what the research found. Answer the way you would if nobody were watching. The point is to catch System 1 at work, not to score well.

Stations to run

All fourteen in order is the whole tour, about thirty minutes. Any one station can run alone. A station card on the home page selects only that station.

Who answers

Projector: bigger type, and the questions wait for you. Take a show of hands, press the answer the class gave, and the reveal follows. Nothing is recorded.

How to run it

  1. Sit somewhere quiet. Two stations use sound: keep earphones nearby.
  2. Read each card once and answer. Do not look anything up and do not use paper unless the card says so.
  3. When a card asks for the first answer that comes to mind, give that one. You will get a chance to check it.
  4. After each station a card shows what happened and what the research found. Read it before you continue.
  5. Esc or Stop ends the run. Finished stations are kept.
