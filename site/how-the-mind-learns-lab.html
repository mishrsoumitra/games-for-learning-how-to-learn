Daniel Willingham · Why Don't Students Like School? · twelve small experiments

How does our mind learn? Try it on yourself.

Daniel Willingham is a cognitive scientist (a fancy name for someone who studies how we think and remember). In his book he doesn't only tell us what the research found. He makes us try it. We read a sentence written in numbers. We look at a list of letters for a few seconds. We try to solve a puzzle about a candle.

This lab has twelve of those small experiments. At each station you do something for two or three minutes, and then the page shows you what happened to you and what happened to the people in the original study. Don't read the Guide first. It gives the answers away.

Stations to do

You can do all twelve in one sitting, or one chapter at a time. A station card on the home page selects only that station.

How to do it

  1. Sit somewhere quiet. Keep your pen, paper and calculator away. Everything is done in your head.
  2. Read each card once and do what it says. Don't look anything up.
  3. Some cards show something for a few seconds and then take it away. Keep your eyes on the screen.
  4. After each station a card shows what happened. Read it before you continue.
  5. Esc or Stop ends the run. The stations you finished are kept.
