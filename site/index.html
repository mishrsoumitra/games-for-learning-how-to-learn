Labs for your own head

Each lab is a set of small tests you run on yourself. Pick the one your lecture asks for. Do it alone, in a quiet place, and write down your score out of 100. Bring it to class.

  1. Attention Lab

    Lecture 4 · also Lecture 5, activities 1 and 2

    How long can you stay on one thing? Measure it four ways: focus stretches, lapses, how steady your reactions are, and how steady your timing is.

  2. Disturbance Lab

    Lecture 5 · slide 7, Remember Seven Digits While I Ruin It

    Remember seven digits under four kinds of sound: silence, loud steady noise, a quiet voice reading numbers, and your own voice kept busy. See what each sound costs you.

  3. Working Memory Lab

    Lecture 5 · The Desk and the Library

    How many things can your working memory hold? Find out, then see what happens when it also has to work, how far chunks stretch it, and how fast it empties.

  4. Thinking, Fast and Slow Lab

    Lecture 5 · slide 9, and homework

    Fourteen small experiments from Kahneman's book. At each one, find out whether your fast System 1 got its way. About thirty minutes for all of them.

  5. Expert and Novice Lab

    Lecture 6 · Chunking

    Two experiments to do on yourself. Sort 24 physics problems into piles, then look at a chess board for five seconds and put the pieces back. See how you did it, and how experts do it.

  6. How the Mind Learns Lab

    Lecture 6 · Chunking, and homework

    Twelve small experiments from Daniel Willingham's book Why Don't Students Like School? Read a sentence written in numbers, remember twenty letters, solve a puzzle about a candle, and see what your own mind does. About thirty-five minutes for all of them.

  7. Study Lab

    Lectures 6, 7 and 8 · Chunking, Retrieval, Spacing · and for your own subjects

    Does the way of studying that feels better work better? Four activities measure it on your own memory: mixed or blocked, reread or recall, cram or space, and a mixed problem set. Then flashcards and mnemonics for your real subjects.

Your results stay on your own phone or laptop. Nothing is sent anywhere. Each lab can export your results as a file.
