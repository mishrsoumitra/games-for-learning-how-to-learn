Seven digits, four kinds of sound. Which one costs you the most?

Seven digits appear one by one and then vanish. You type them back in order. That is the whole task, and you do it four times. Only the sound in the room changes: silence, a loud steady noise, a quiet voice reading random numbers, and then your own voice saying a times table. Before you start, write down your guess: which round will cost you the most digits?

For each list, the digits appear one at a time and vanish. You type them back in order. After every list you see what you typed next to what was on the board. Between rounds a card tells you what changes. Use earphones or a speaker, and turn the volume up before you start.

How the digits appear

Every study of this effect shows the digits one at a time. The five-second board is for the projector and the slide.

Who answers

Projector: the screen shows the digits, plays the sound, gives 20 seconds to write, then reveals the answer for self-scoring. Nothing is recorded.

Sound check

Turn the noise up until it is annoying: about as loud as a busy road, not painful. The research used levels from 60 to 95 dB(A).

The voice uses your device's built-in speech. Play it once here to make sure it works.

Round 4 · what you say out loud

How to run it

  1. Earphones in or speaker on, volume up. Sit somewhere you can talk out loud for Round 4.
  2. Before you start, write down your guess: which round will cost you the most digits?
  3. Watch the digits. Do not write anything down and do not say them, not even under your breath.
  4. When they vanish, type them in order on the keypad (or your keyboard) and press Enter. Leave a gap blank if you have lost it: just type what you have.
  5. After each list you see the board next to what you typed. A digit in red came from the voice, not from the board.
  6. Between rounds a card tells you what is about to change. Nothing else does.
