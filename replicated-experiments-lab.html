Fourteen experiments that held up when they were run again

Don't take the textbook's word for it. Run the experiment on yourself.

Some famous experiments in psychology gave a result once and never again. Others have been run again and again, in many countries, by people who were hoping to catch a mistake, and the result came out the same every time.

This lab has fourteen of the second kind. Each one is a small game of three to five minutes. You play it, and then the page draws your own result next to the result in the published study. Don't read the Guide first. It gives the answers away.

Most of the time it does. When it doesn't, the card tells you why one short run can go the other way.

Fourteen stations, in three parts

The stations · one part takes fifteen to twenty-five minutes

Fourteen experiments to run on yourself

A card tells you what to do. You do it. Then the page draws your result next to the published one. Nothing here is a test of how clever you are. If a station goes badly for you, that is usually the point of the station.

Stations to do

All fourteen take about an hour, so do one part at a time. Tap the name of a part to select only that part. A station card on the home page selects only that station.

How to do it

  1. Sit somewhere quiet, where you can also say a few words aloud. Keep pen and paper away.
  2. A laptop is best for the fast stations: use the left and right arrow keys. On a phone, use both thumbs.
  3. Some cards show words for a second and take them away. Keep your eyes on the screen and don't switch tabs.
  4. After each station a card shows your data next to the study's. Read it before you continue.
  5. Esc or Stop ends the run. The stations you finished are kept.
